Class of 2025

  • Phil Ciccarelli

    Maddog alumnus and UMass head coach

    A Maddog alumnus who went on to build UMass Amherst into a national contender as their head coach, taking them to the D1AA final four in 2024.

  • Andrew Hermo

    Original 1984 Maddog and FONUR board member

    One of the original Maddogs, Andy played center from 1984 to 1989 and was one of the pivotal players in the club’s first run for legitimacy. Solid in defense, he used his co-ops in New Jersey to play for the Union men’s team and improve his game. After graduation and a long corporate career, he has served on the FONUR alumni board for 15 years, as treasurer, vice president and Giving Day fundraising lead.

  • Derrick Isoka

    USA Sevens pool player

    Derrick joined the Maddogs as a freshman and represented the club over several seasons before ten years with Boston RFC in the Super League, captaining their sevens and tens sides. In 2007 he was selected for the USA Sevens pool (a first for a Boston player) for the New Zealand, Australia and Hong Kong legs of the IRB circuit, and in 2011 he captained Boston to the Hell’s Gate Sevens, NRU and NERFU titles. He later played for Golden Gate in San Francisco, including a match against Tonga, before retiring in 2014.

  • Michael "Tree" Roy

    Lock in the 1995 undefeated team

    Class of 1996

    After a four-year rowing career at NU that included a 1994 national title, Mike joined the Maddogs for his senior year and at 6’7” quickly became one of the most intimidating locks in Division II. He anchored the set piece for the undefeated, untried-upon fall 1995 side and the spring Beast of the East and Beanpot titles. He then starred for Boston RFC through five Super League playoff seasons and played a year of first grade in Sydney.

  • Jason "Babyface" Trenouth

    Fly half of the mid-90s revival

    A walk-on to NU’s Division I soccer team who needed more contact in his life and joined the Maddogs. His first game was the 1994 Beanpot, where Division I Harvard and BC beat the Division II Maddogs, and by 1995 he had taken command of the fly half position. His tactical kicking helped the 1995 Maddogs go undefeated and untried upon on the way to the D2 Northeast Championship. He was Man of the Match in the 1996 Beanpot final, scoring every point against BU, and kicked the winning points in the final minute of the Beast of the East final. He then played ten seasons for Boston RFC, later serving as their Super League athletic trainer.

Class of 2023

2012–13
Team

The 2012 team: The 2012–13 CRC sevens team

Coached by Dave Wedge.

  • Collegiate Rugby Championships bowl winners (spring 2013)
  • Cianci Sevens champions (spring 2013)

The Maddogs’ first College Rugby Championships sevens side won the 2012 ECRC Sevens, the national sevens qualifier, and the Cianci Sevens, finished 13th at the 2012 USA Sevens Nationals, then won the 2013 UNH Sevens and the Bowl at the 2013 Collegiate Rugby Championships, with a nationally televised appearance on NBC.

  • JP "Boone" Bonin

    Captain, president and player-coach through the mid-90s

    Class of 1991

    Boone saw the club through a hard transition in the mid-90s, after the first nationally ranked eras had run their course and the team lost its homes at the Triangle and Magazine Beach. He served as captain, club president and player-coach, and coached the club full time for a year after graduating. He went on to play in USA Rugby’s Super League for Washington RFC and served as its president from 2000 to 2004. In 2012 he returned to help FONUR with the Wallin Endowment and helped secure the Maddogs’ invitation to the nationally televised 2013 Collegiate Rugby Championship.

  • Ryan Casey

    Flanker, three-season captain and coach

    Class of 2001

    A hard-hitting flanker who helped the Maddogs reach the 1998 Beast of the East final and win the 1998 Beanpot, and started for the Northeast All-Stars on their 1998 tour. He captained the club for three seasons, then recruited fellow alumni as assistants and took over as head coach in 2000 and 2001, leading the club to the East Coast playoffs both falls. In 2017 he founded the rugby program at Weymouth High School and has sent several players on to Division I college rugby.

Class of 2022

  • Nino Balduzzi

    Wing, captain and club president

    Class of 2001

    “Nino was the only player I can recall that had the potential to score any time he got the ball.” A New England Collegiate All-Star from 1998 to 2001 and the club’s captain and president in his final two years. He played 11 seasons for Boston RFC, was part of the US Club Northeast select side from 2002 to 2008 and the National 7s all-star competition, and was invited to the US National 7s tryout camp.

  • Tim Cummings

    Original Maddog, scrum half and captain

    Class of 1990

    An A-side starter his entire career who led the scrum and dictated the pace of play with a great rugby IQ. All-New England and All-Collegiate ERU, an ERU champion at fly half, and captain in his senior year. Afterwards he played scrum half for Beacon Hill and Boston in two national Sweet Sixteen tournaments.

  • Chris Frazier

    Captain, All-Conference and New England Free Jack

    Class of 2014

    Chris came to NU on a football scholarship and, when the program folded after his freshman year, joined the Maddogs and was captain by his second season. ECRC All-Conference in 2013 and 2014, he played for the Northeast Olympic Development Academy and was selected for the USA Eagles 7s High Performance camp. He won club national titles with Mystic River in 2016 and 2018, played for the USA Falcons, and in 2018 signed with the New England Free Jacks, starting at number eight in their debut. He won bronze with Mystic River at the USA Rugby Club 7s championships in 2015 and 2018, and became the Maddogs’ assistant backs coach in 2020.

  • Jim Grace

    Captain of the undefeated 1990 team

    Class of 1990

    A transfer from Missouri, where he played Division I football, Jim captained the undefeated 1990 Maddogs from openside flanker. A relentless tackler with deceptive pace in the open field, he was a quiet, humble captain who led by example, and a New England Collegiate All-Star. He went on to a distinguished career with the DEA.

  • Rudy Machacek

    Founding member and nationally ranked lock

    Class of 1988

    One of the founding Maddogs. At 6’5” he was a force in the set piece and the loose: a New England Collegiate and Collegiate ERU All-Star who started every match of the New England Collegiate 15s tour of New Zealand and Australia. He was Man of the Match for the ERU under-25s against Saracens U-21 at the Boston International Tournament and was invited to the Junior Eagles camp.

  • Robert "Ogre" McCarthy

    Inside center and the man who got rugby recognised at NU

    Class of 1989

    A West Point transfer after his freshman year, Ogre recruited Professor Peter Eastman as the team’s advisor, which led to the club’s official recognition at Northeastern. A starting inside center (and a prop when injuries demanded it), he was a New England Collegiate All-Star and toured Australia and New Zealand with the New England Collegiate 15s. He went on to serve with distinction in the US Marine Corps, playing for the USMC and Combined Services sides, and retired as a Colonel in 2020.

Class of 2019

1989–90
Team

The 1989 team: The 1990 undefeated team

  • Undefeated season

The first team after the original Maddogs graduated, leaving a big void and a rebuild, went the whole season undefeated under captain Jim Grace. They were inducted as the second Hall of Fame team on the club’s 35th anniversary.

  • Jay Dacey

    Coach, 1985–1988

    Jay first noticed the Maddogs at the New England championships while playing for his club, Mystic River. He saw the size of the Maddog pack and, with the club self-coached at the time, decided to help out. He coached the Maddogs from 1985 to 1988, alongside a long playing career with Mystic River that included national championships.

  • Eric Graham

    FONUR fundraising lead

  • Rich Hilton

    Fly half, coach and FONUR president

    Rich came to NU from England in 1983 as an international student. Having played rugby at high school in England, he was one of the few who understood the game, and quickly became the backs coach while the team coached itself until Jay Dacey arrived. A fly half and field general, he was known for putting up high kicks for the centers to chase, and played for White Plains on co-op in New York and briefly for Sale Sharks on co-op in his hometown of Manchester.

    After graduating he played for Old Gold, Beacon Hill and Boston RFC, and returned to coach the Maddogs with Eric Graham and Mark Phillips in the mid to late 1990s while coaching the Boston RFC backs in the Super League. As Boston’s head coach he took the team to the national Division 1 quarter-finals, and their third side won the Division 3 men’s national title in 2000. He later coached the Ventura Outlaws from Division 4 to Division 2 in successive seasons, and took California Lutheran University to the CRAA national D2 final in his third year.

Class of 2018

1987–88
Team

The 1987 team: The Ten Ton Scrum

  • New England Collegiate Division 1 playoffs

The Ten Ton Scrum of 1988 competed in New England Collegiate Division 1 against powerhouses like Dartmouth, Harvard and BC, and didn’t just compete but dominated, thanks in large part to a forward pack that was probably the biggest in New England, men’s clubs included. Their goal was always to have an opposition player leave the field after the opening kickoff.

Their only regular-season loss was by three points to Dartmouth, who went on to be national runners-up. The Maddogs made the playoffs and lost a tough battle against West Point in New York. They were the first team inducted into the Hall of Fame.

  • Robert Eastman

    Academic advisor who won the club university recognition

    The club’s academic advisor, who helped the Maddogs gain official recognition from the university.

  • Bob Hubbard

    Founder of the Maddogs

    Bob founded the Maddogs. He called the first meeting on the steps of the Cabot gym in 1983 and organized the first practices and games in 1984. His playing career was cut short when he broke his back in the club’s first game, against New England College. Bob left NU early and went on to a long and distinguished career with the FBI.

  • Professor Bruce Wallin

    Faculty friend and namesake of the Wallin Endowment

    As a student, Bruce Wallin played for the Ivy League championship rugby team of 1969, but in the end he was a Maddog at heart. He became the club’s first academic advisor, in an unofficial way, and to recognize his contribution the alumni named the endowment funds after him.
