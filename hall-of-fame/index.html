Class of 2025

  • Phil Ciccarelli

    Maddog alumnus and UMass head coach

    A Maddog alumnus who went on to build UMass Amherst into a national contender as their head coach, taking them to the D1AA final four in 2024.

  • Andrew Hermo

    Original 1984 Maddog and FONUR board member

    One of the original Maddogs, Andy played center from 1984 to 1989 and was one of the pivotal players in the club’s first run for legitimacy. Solid in defense, he used his co-ops in New Jersey to play for the Union men’s team and improve his game. After graduation and a long corporate career, he has served on the FONUR alumni board for 15 years, as treasurer, vice president and on Giving Day fundraising communications.

  • Derrick Isoka

    USA Sevens pool player

    Derrick joined the Maddogs as a freshman and represented the club over several seasons before ten years with Boston RFC in the Super League, captaining their sevens and tens sides. In 2007 he was selected for the USA Sevens pool (a first for a Boston player) for the New Zealand, Australia and Hong Kong legs of the IRB circuit, and in 2011 he captained Boston to the Hell’s Gate Sevens, NRU and NERFU titles. He later played for Golden Gate in San Francisco, including a match against Tonga, before retiring in 2014.

  • Michael "Tree" Roy

    Lock in the 1995 undefeated team

    Class of 1996

    After a four-year rowing career at NU that included a 1994 national title, Mike joined the Maddogs for his senior year and at 6’7” quickly became one of the most intimidating locks in Division II. He anchored the set piece for the undefeated, untried-upon fall 1995 side and the spring Beast of the East and Beanpot titles. He then starred for Boston RFC through five Super League playoff seasons and played a year of first grade in Sydney.

  • Jason "Babyface" Trenouth

    Fly half of the mid-90s revival

    A walk-on to NU’s Division I soccer team who needed more contact in his life and joined the Maddogs. His first game was the 1994 Beanpot, where Division I Harvard and BC beat the Division II Maddogs, and by 1995 he had taken command of the fly half position. His tactical kicking helped the 1995 Maddogs go undefeated and untried upon on the way to the D2 Northeast Championship. He was Man of the Match in the 1996 Beanpot final, scoring every point against BU, and kicked the winning points in the final minute of the Beast of the East final. He then played ten seasons for Boston RFC, later serving as their Super League athletic trainer.

Class of 2023

2012–13
Team

The 2012 team: The 2012–13 CRC sevens team

Coached by Dave Wedge.

  • Collegiate Rugby Championships bowl winners (spring 2013)
  • Cianci Sevens champions (spring 2013)
  • UNH Sevens champions (spring 2013)

The Maddogs’ first College Rugby Championships sevens side won the 2012 ECRC Sevens, the national sevens qualifier, and the Cianci Sevens, finished 13th at the 2012 USA Sevens Nationals, then won the 2013 UNH Sevens and the Bowl at the 2013 Collegiate Rugby Championships, with a nationally televised appearance on NBC.

The side was captained by club president Ty Taylor, with Diego Maquieira as vice president, and coached by Jamie Green, Greg Bruce and Adam Zilcoski; Dimitri Efthimiou made the CRC All-Tournament Team. Athletic director Peter Roby personally vouched for the program to the organizers, the club sold well over the 300 tickets the CRC required, and the Northeastern section was among the rowdiest in the stadium all weekend.

  • JP "Boone" Bonin

    Captain, president and player-coach through the mid-90s

    Class of 1991

    Boone saw the club through a hard transition in the mid-90s, after the first nationally ranked eras had run their course and the team lost its homes at the Triangle and Magazine Beach. He served as captain, club president and player-coach, and coached the club full time for a year after graduating. He went on to play in USA Rugby’s Super League for Washington RFC and served as its president from 2000 to 2004. In 2012 he returned to help FONUR with the Wallin Endowment and helped secure the Maddogs’ invitation to the nationally televised 2013 Collegiate Rugby Championship.

  • Ryan Casey

    Flanker, three-season captain and coach

    Class of 2001

    A hard-hitting flanker who helped the Maddogs reach the 1998 Beast of the East final and win the 1998 Beanpot, and started for the Northeast All-Stars on their 1998 tour. He captained the club for three seasons, then recruited fellow alumni as assistants and took over as head coach in 2000 and 2001, leading the club to the East Coast playoffs both falls. In 2017 he founded the rugby program at Weymouth High School and has sent several players on to Division I college rugby.

  • Jon Hagan

    Forward and president of the 1995 Criminals

    Class of 1997

    After the Maddogs lost their old grounds at the Triangle of Death and Magazine Beach, low numbers forced the club down to Division 2 in the fall of 1993. In 1994 the students negotiated their own permits and found a new home at Madison Park High School, and under club president Jon Hagan and alumni coaches Mark Phillips, Eric Graham and Rich Hilton the squad swelled to more than fifty players. A forward in that pack, Jon was president of the fall 1995 side that won the New England Division 2 title, the original Criminals, who went on to lose only a handful of games over the next six seasons.

  • George Johnson

    Hall of Fame inductee

    Class of 1985

  • Diego Maquieira

    Hooker, CRC sevens vice president and Major League Rugby pro

    Class of 2014

    Diego was vice president of the 2012–13 club and played in its first Collegiate Rugby Championships sevens side, alongside president and captain Ty Taylor. That team won the ECRC Sevens and the Cianci Sevens, played at the 2012 USA Sevens Nationals, won the 2013 UNH Sevens and took the Bowl at the 2013 Collegiate Rugby Championships. Athletic director Peter Roby personally called the organizers to vouch for the program, the club sold well over the 300 tickets the CRC required, and the Northeastern section was among the rowdiest in the stadium. The team was inducted into the Hall of Fame in 2023, the same year as Diego.

    A Queens native who came to Northeastern from Bucknell, where he had gone to play football, Diego graduated in 2014 and kept climbing. At hooker for Mystic River he was a prominent member of the side that won the 2016 USA Rugby Club Division 1 national championship, and he played sevens for the Northeast 7s Academy. In 2017 he signed with the Houston Strikers, who became the Houston SaberCats, and played seven matches in Major League Rugby’s inaugural 2018 season. He joined the New England Free Jacks in 2019 and Rugby United New York in 2021.

  • Elliot Rizzo

    Back and co-captain of the undefeated 2010 spring side

    Class of 2011

    Elliot co-captained the spring 2010 Maddogs with Jeff Solimine. Coming off a winless season the year before, the side went undefeated, conceded fewer than 30 points all spring with shutouts like 49–0 over UConn and 41–0 over BU, sent three players to NRU selection, and started players from five countries. They won the Beast of the East, beating Brown in the final, and then beat the alumni at Alumni Weekend for good measure.

  • Dave Wedge

    FONUR president for a decade

    1993–1998 · Lock, Blindside flanker, Openside flanker · Coached 2012–2022

    Dave led FONUR as president for about ten years, seeing the Wallin Endowment through to activation and building up the Giving Day campaigns and the alumni weekends that bring Maddogs of every era back together.

Class of 2022

  • Nino Balduzzi

    Wing, captain and club president

    Class of 2001

    “Nino was the only player I can recall that had the potential to score any time he got the ball.” A New England Collegiate All-Star from 1998 to 2001 and the club’s captain and president in his final two years. He played 11 seasons for Boston RFC, was part of the US Club Northeast select side from 2002 to 2008 and the National 7s all-star competition, and was invited to the US National 7s tryout camp.

  • Tim Cummings

    Original Maddog, scrum half and captain

    Class of 1990

    An A-side starter his entire career who led the scrum and dictated the pace of play with a great rugby IQ. All-New England and All-Collegiate ERU, an ERU champion at fly half, and captain in his senior year. Afterwards he played scrum half for Beacon Hill and Boston in two national Sweet Sixteen tournaments.

  • Chris Frazier

    Captain, All-Conference and New England Free Jack

    Class of 2014

    Chris came to NU on a football scholarship and, when the program folded after his freshman year, joined the Maddogs and was captain by his second season. ECRC All-Conference in 2013 and 2014, he played for the Northeast Olympic Development Academy and was selected for the USA Eagles 7s High Performance camp. He won club national titles with Mystic River in 2016 and 2018, played for the USA Falcons, and in 2018 signed with the New England Free Jacks, starting at number eight in their debut. He won bronze with Mystic River at the USA Rugby Club 7s championships in 2015 and 2018, and became the Maddogs’ assistant backs coach in 2020.

  • Jim Grace

    Captain of the undefeated 1990 team

    Class of 1990

    A transfer from Missouri, where he played Division I football, Jim captained the undefeated 1990 Maddogs from openside flanker. A relentless tackler with deceptive pace in the open field, he was a quiet, humble captain who led by example, and a New England Collegiate All-Star. He went on to a distinguished career with the DEA.

  • Rudy Machacek

    Founding member and nationally ranked lock

    Class of 1988

    One of the founding Maddogs. At 6’5” he was a force in the set piece and the loose: a New England Collegiate and Collegiate ERU All-Star who started every match of the New England Collegiate 15s tour of New Zealand and Australia. He was Man of the Match for the ERU under-25s against Saracens U-21 at the Boston International Tournament and was invited to the Junior Eagles camp.

  • Robert "Ogre" McCarthy

    Inside center and the man who got rugby recognised at NU

    Class of 1989

    A West Point transfer after his freshman year, Ogre recruited Professor Peter Eastman as the team’s advisor, which led to the club’s official recognition at Northeastern. A starting inside center (and a prop when injuries demanded it), he was a New England Collegiate All-Star and toured Australia and New Zealand with the New England Collegiate 15s. He went on to serve with distinction in the US Marine Corps, playing for the USMC and Combined Services sides, and retired as a Colonel in 2020.

Class of 2019

1989–90
Team

The 1989 team: The 1990 undefeated team

Captain Mark Phillips. 3 players.

  • Undefeated season
  • 10 Good Men tournament champions (August 1989)
  • New England Division 1 champions (fall 1989)
  • Beanpot champions (spring 1990)
  • Beast of the East runners-up (spring 1990)

The first team after the original Maddogs graduated, leaving a big void and a rebuild, went the whole season undefeated under captain Jim Grace. They were inducted as the second Hall of Fame team on the club’s 35th anniversary.

Arguably the most successful single year in club history. They won the 10 Good Men tournament in August 1989, went unbeaten through the fall to finish first in New England Division 1, were ranked as high as 13th in the country, and reached the semifinals of the Northeast Championships. In the spring they won the 1990 Beanpot and were runners-up at the Beast of the East, losing the final to Brown by two points.

  • Jay Dacey

    Coach, 1985–1988

    Jay first noticed the Maddogs at the New England championships while playing for his club, Mystic River. He saw the size of the Maddog pack and, with the club self-coached at the time, decided to help out. He coached the Maddogs from 1985 to 1988, alongside a long playing career with Mystic River that included national championships.

  • Eric Graham

    Center, fullback, coach and founder of FONUR

    1986–1989 · Inside centre, Outside centre, Right wing, Fullback · Coached 1994–1998

    Eric played center and fullback for the Maddogs from 1986 to 1990, through the Ten Ton Scrum era, and returned to coach from 1994 to 1998 with Rich Hilton and Mark Phillips during the Criminals’ run of New England titles. He also informally founded FONUR in 1995 and served as its president for its first 15 years.

    After his Maddog playing career, Eric played briefly for Old Blue in New York City as starting fullback, before moving to Italy for work. At the time, Italy ran the only professional rugby union league in the world, and many legends of the game went there to be paid to play; it wasn’t until 1995 that rugby union was allowed to go professional worldwide. Eric lived and worked near Rovigo, a small city about an hour south of Venice. It was a hotbed for rugby, and Rovigo remains one of the top professional teams in Italy.

    When he showed up to practice with his boots, Rovigo Rugby invited him to join the team for its 1992 preseason. Soon after, several foreign players arrived, including Naas Botha (flyhalf and captain of the South African Springboks) and Julian Gardner and Willie O (both forwards capped for the Australian Wallabies). The Rovigo coaches recognized that Eric had some skills and put him on for the second half of their first preseason match. After he scored two tries in that half to win the game, Rovigo invited him to join the squad full time, and he played alongside these rugby legends for the next two years.

    It was a truly amazing time in his life, and the world of rugby opened up to him. Rugby was a global community, and he learned the game’s technical skills at the highest level.

    In 1994 he brought that experience home to the Maddogs, encouraging Mark Phillips and Rich Hilton to coach the team with him. Although the three of them were volunteers, the team still needed funds to operate, so in 1995 he started fundraising for the Maddogs and building the foundation of FONUR.

    He spearheaded the creation of the Bruce Wallin Endowment with Jon Hagan, who had taken over as FONUR’s president, and updated the alumni as the fund neared its goal. Over the years, Eric has helped the Maddogs raise close to $1,000,000.

    In 2023, Eric brought his fundraising skills to help Maddogs coach Ed Tubridy launch the NCR All-American team. He and Ed, supported by many Maddog alumni, raised over $100,000 and brought the 2023 All-Americans to Bordeaux for the WURIT tournament, where they took fourth place, losing in the semi-finals to the Collegiate All Blacks from New Zealand. The NCR All-Americans have since become a full annual program with NCR, with Ed still serving as head coach.

    Eric and Ed are committed to having Northeastern University host the WURIT tournament when it is played in 2031 alongside the Rugby World Cup.

  • Rich Hilton

    Fly half, coach and FONUR president

    1984–1988 · Fly-half · Coached 1994–1998

    • MVP Back, 1987

    Rich came to NU from England in 1983 as an international student. Having played rugby at high school in England, he was one of the few who understood the game, and quickly became the backs coach while the team coached itself until Jay Dacey arrived. A fly half and field general, he was known for putting up high kicks for the centers to chase, and played for White Plains on co-op in New York and briefly for Sale Sharks on co-op in his hometown of Manchester.

    After graduating he played for Old Gold, Beacon Hill and Boston RFC, and returned to coach the Maddogs with Eric Graham and Mark Phillips in the mid to late 1990s while coaching the Boston RFC backs in the Super League. As Boston’s head coach he took the team to the national Division 1 quarter-finals, and their third side won the Division 3 men’s national title in 2000. He later coached the Ventura Outlaws from Division 4 to Division 2 in successive seasons, and took California Lutheran University to the CRAA national D2 final in his third year.

  • Mark "Phil" Phillips

    Number eight and coach

    1986–1989 · Number 8, Openside flanker · Coached 1995–2012

    A 2019 Rugby Hall of Fame inductee in the second-ever Hall of Fame class, Mark Phillips has built a distinguished rugby career defined by championship success, representative honors, leadership, and a lifelong commitment to developing the game. A national All-Star champion, a standout in New England Division I club rugby, and a coach of championship-winning teams at both the collegiate and high school levels, Phillips has made a lasting impact across more than three decades of rugby.

    As a player, Phillips helped Boston Rugby Club capture six consecutive NERFU championships from 1996 to 2001, reach the National Elite Eight in 2000, and become one of the original teams selected for USA Rugby’s inaugural Super League season. He represented USA Rugby East at the senior level, winning the national All-Star Championship in 1994, and earned selection to the inaugural NERFU Collegiate Select XV tour of New Zealand and Australia in 1988. As a coach, he guided Northeastern University RFC to a Division II national runner-up finish in 1997 and later helped lead Weymouth High School Rugby to three consecutive Division II State Championships from 2022 through 2024.

    Phillips’ rugby career began at Northeastern University, where he played from 1986 to 1989 while earning a Bachelor of Science in Business Administration. He captained the team during the 1988–1989 season, helping Northeastern reach the NERFU Championship final in 1987 and win the NERFU Championship in 1989 before finishing as national Sweet Sixteen runner-up that same year. Primarily a number eight and open-side flanker, Phillips earned selection in 1988 to the NERFU Collegiate Select XV as one of only 25 players chosen from 150 invited Division I and Division II players across New England. The inaugural month-long tour of New Zealand and Australia was sponsored by Reebok and organized under Harvard head coach Martyn Kingston. While in New Zealand, the team trained under New Zealand Rugby Football Union coaches and All Blacks Director of Coaching Bill Freeman. Phillips also represented USA Rugby East at the collegiate level, reaching the 1988 Collegiate ITT semifinals and touring Nova Scotia and Newfoundland in 1989.

    Following graduation, Phillips established himself as a leading forward in New England Division I club rugby. He played for Beacon Hill RFC from 1989 to 1996, helping the club win four consecutive NERFU championships from 1993 through 1996 and reach the NRU final in 1995. After Beacon Hill merged with Boston Rugby Club in 1996, he continued with Boston RFC through 2001. During his tenure, Boston captured six straight NERFU championships, advanced to the National Sweet Sixteen in 1996 and the Elite Eight in 2000, and earned a place among the original clubs selected for USA Rugby’s inaugural Super League season.

    Phillips’ representative career further distinguished his playing accomplishments. He represented the NERFU Senior XV from 1990 to 1997 and served as captain from 1996 to 1997. His achievements included an NRU Sub-Union finalist finish in 1993, a championship in 1994, a tour of Quebec in 1996, and a match against Pontypridd, the 1997 Welsh National League champions, featuring Welsh internationals Neil Jenkins and Dale McIntosh. In 1993 and 1994, Phillips was also selected for USA Rugby East’s Senior XV, a 25-player panel representing the Eastern Rugby Union in the national All-Star Championship against the Midwest, West and Pacific regional territories. The team placed third in 1993 before capturing the national title in 1994.

    Phillips carried his competitive experience into coaching, returning to his alma mater as head coach of Northeastern University RFC from 1996 to 1998 and again from 2009 to 2012. During his first tenure, he helped transform the program from Division III to Division I in just two seasons. In 1997, Northeastern won the Beast of the East, Beanpot, NERFU Division II, and NRU Division II championships, culminating in a Division II national runner-up finish. The team earned promotion to Division I in 1998. Returning to the program more than a decade later, Phillips led Northeastern to another Beast of the East championship in 2010 and an undefeated ECRC championship season in 2011, allowing only two tries throughout the entire season. In 2012, the team advanced to the USA Rugby National Tournament Sweet Sixteen.

    In 2000, Phillips also served as assistant/player coach for Boston RFC, contributing to a championship season in which the club won the NERFU, NRU, and ERU titles and reached the USARFU National Championship Elite Eight.

    His commitment to developing rugby continued at the high school level as an assistant coach for Weymouth High School RFC from 2022 to 2024. During those three seasons, Weymouth compiled a 21–3 record and won three consecutive Division II State Championships, in 2022, 2023, and 2024.

    From collegiate captain and national All-Star champion to Division I club standout and championship-winning coach, Phillips’ induction into the Rugby Hall of Fame in 2019 recognizes a career of sustained achievement and service to the sport. His legacy extends beyond championships and representative honors to the teams he helped build, the players he mentored, and the generations of rugby players he has inspired.

Class of 2018

1987–88
Team

The 1987 team: The Ten Ton Scrum

3 players.

  • New England Collegiate Division 1 playoffs

The Ten Ton Scrum of 1988 competed in New England Collegiate Division 1 against powerhouses like Dartmouth, Harvard and BC, and didn’t just compete but dominated, thanks in large part to a forward pack that was probably the biggest in New England, men’s clubs included. Their goal was always to have an opposition player leave the field after the opening kickoff.

The club was accepted into the New England Rugby Football Union in 1987, with Jay Dacey of Mystic River as volunteer coach and Steve Richter and Tony Kalaijakis captaining the backs and forwards. The self-funded side ran on ingenuity, and its legend grew: freakish size, an exceptional team GPA, a persistent rumour that the props were thirty-year-old inmates, and, by their own account, never a lost scrum.

Their only regular-season loss was by three points to Dartmouth, who went on to be national runners-up. The Maddogs made the playoffs and lost a tough battle against West Point in New York. They were the first team inducted into the Hall of Fame.

  • Robert Eastman

    Academic advisor who won the club university recognition

    The club’s academic advisor, who helped the Maddogs gain official recognition from the university.

  • Bob Hubbard

    Founder of the Maddogs

    Bob founded the Maddogs. He called the first meeting on the steps of the Cabot gym in 1983 and organized the first practices and games in 1984. His playing career was cut short when he broke his back in the club’s first game, against New England College. Bob left NU early and went on to a long and distinguished career with the FBI.

  • Professor Bruce Wallin

    Faculty friend and namesake of the Wallin Endowment

    As a student, Bruce Wallin played for the Ivy League championship rugby team of 1969, but in the end he was a Maddog at heart. He became the club’s first academic advisor, in an unofficial way, and to recognize his contribution the alumni named the endowment funds after him.
